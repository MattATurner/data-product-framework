Specification-Driven Data Products on Google Cloud

Executive Summary

Over the past eighteen months, enterprise data teams have encountered a frustrating paradox. Generative AI assistants can draft a hundred lines of SQL in ten seconds, yet taking a new business idea all the way to a trusted, governed production data product still takes months. Speeding up SQL typing inside a fragmented delivery process simply moves teams faster toward user-acceptance rework, security review delays, and reconciliation disputes with Finance.

This whitepaper introduces the Data Product Framework (DPF), an open, specification-driven operating model and reference implementation for Google Cloud. Rather than asking engineers to hand-stitch five separate cloud tools on every project—or allowing unconstrained coding agents to guess business rules in the dark—DPF separates what machines do deterministically from what only humans can decide. Organisations encode their institutional context once in a shared registry, capture each business need in plain everyday English, and compile every cloud plane offline in seconds between six explicit human decision gates.

At enterprise scale, build-time compilation is paired symbiotically with the Agentic Data Governance Framework (AGF)—a companion runtime reference architecture on Gemini Platform that governs live cloud estates from table birth through seven-year retirement across all fourteen EDM Council Cloud Data Management Capabilities (CDMC). Using BigQuery Knowledge Catalog and seven single-writer metadata aspects as a zero-coupling contract bus, DPF enforces privacy, lineage, and semantic invariants before code ever ships—while AGF guardians continuously verify live table births, purpose-based access, general-ledger reconciliation, and regulatory retention across the cloud estate.

In our reference architecture, a single validated plain-English specification and concise source-typing SQL compile deterministically in under two seconds into complete BigQuery and Dataform (or dbt) transformation pipelines, row-level quarantine gates, Terraform IAM and dynamic masking policies, isolated partner BigQuery sharing listings, BigQuery Knowledge Catalog quality scans, and symmetric-aggregate-safe Looker Explores and dashboards—compressing weeks of cross-team plumbing into hours while raising governance rigour.

1. The Enterprise Data Delivery Paradox

1.1 Why Faster SQL Has Not Produced Faster Outcomes

When data leaders audit where calendar time actually disappears between an initial business request and a trusted executive dashboard, writing transformation SQL rarely accounts for more than ten percent of elapsed effort. Across industries, quarters vanish into four structural bottlenecks:

  1. Unscored portfolio intake: Engineering capacity is consumed debating vague requests or building low-return pipelines because teams lack a lightweight, objective way to score commercial value against data readiness upfront.
  2. Translation ping-pong between business and engineering: Requirements are captured either as wishful slide decks or as spreadsheets of raw source column names, leaving critical commercial edge cases—such as mid-year customer reclassifications, cancelled orders, and restatement windows—undiscovered until user acceptance testing.
  3. Manual five-plane stitching: Delivering a single governed data product requires coordinating transformation SQL, infrastructure and IAM, column-level PII masking, catalog metadata and quality rules, and BI semantic models across disconnected repositories and teams.
  4. Green syntax, wrong business numbers: Pipelines pass technical continuous-integration checks while publishing figures that disagree with Finance or Merchandising because automated tests check syntax and nullability rather than signed business semantics.

1.2 Why Unbounded Prompt-Driven Pipelines Stall at Scale

With the arrival of autonomous coding agents and engine-native assistants, many teams hope to bypass those bottlenecks by prompting an agent directly from a chat request to a deployed pipeline. While effective for one-off sandbox exploration, prompt-driven pipelines stall in enterprise production for three reasons:

  1. Agents lack your institutional context: Generic models know BigQuery and Terraform syntax, yet they do not know that your business day runs strictly during regional trading hours, that an isolated operational database allows only periodic outbound extracts, which domains share a single conformed definition of Customer, or which columns require dynamic PII masking. Without structured context, engineers re-explain institutional rules in prompts and pull-request comments on every project.
  2. Database jargon leaks into business sign-off: When engineers or agents ask business sponsors to approve documents filled with terms like table grain, surrogate keys, or Slowly Changing Dimensions Type 2, sponsors sign documents they cannot meaningfully verify.
  3. Multi-plane drift accelerates: Generating SQL ten times faster without deterministic coordination across Terraform, BigQuery Knowledge Catalog, and Looker simply creates ten times more ungoverned tables, missing policy tags, and shadow BI derived tables.

2. The 6 Human-Gated Operating Model

To compress delivery from months to hours and days without sacrificing trust, DPF separates one-time portfolio funding from a continuous five-stage product lifecycle. Deterministic checks and bounded AI skills handle ninety-five percent of preparation between stages, while leaders step in strictly at six clear decision gates—and ongoing business evolution loops straight back into Step 2 rather than restarting portfolio intake.

Step 1: Prioritise — Turn Business Ambition into a Funded Use-Case Brief

High-impact data products rarely originate inside an engineering backlog. They emerge from structured business innovation workshops, quarterly commercial reviews, frontline operational bottlenecks, and executive value-discovery sessions targeting concrete levers—growing customer revenue, reducing operational cost and leakage, automating manual risk decisions, or unlocking external partner insights. Left unstructured, however, those workshops generate dozens of competing wishlist ideas where the loudest voice in the room wins engineering capacity rather than the highest-return opportunity.

Step 1 channels that raw stream of ideas into a disciplined opportunity funnel before a single engineer is assigned. Sponsors capture each candidate opportunity as a concise two-page Funded Use-Case Brief focused strictly on the commercial decision at stake, measurable upside, target consumers, and believed source systems—giving leadership an apples-to-apples view across revenue generation, cost reduction, and risk-mitigation bets.

Deterministic & Agentic Automation
  1. The Readiness Check agent evaluates the two-page submission against an eleven-criterion evidence rubric before the scoring panel meets, flagging vague value claims, unstated consumers, or honest source-readiness unknowns as clear questions for the sponsor.
Human Decision Gate

At the Portfolio Gate, business and data leaders score the candidate funnel across eleven weighted Value and Ease criteria in a single session—approving high-return Quick Wins as a Funded Use-Case Brief, sequencing larger Strategic Bets, and parking low-return requests before engineering begins.

Step 2: Describe — Capture Plain-English Rules in a Business Requirements Document (Gate G0)

Once a candidate opportunity clears the portfolio funnel, the greatest threat to delivery velocity is premature technical translation. In most organisations, discovery sessions immediately drift into database schemas, raw source column names, and cloud modelling terminology—forcing business sponsors to nod along to technical specifications they cannot genuinely verify while leaving critical commercial edge cases unspoken until months later in user acceptance testing.

Step 2 keeps requirements anchored strictly in how the business operates and measures success. Product owners, domain subject-matter experts, and business analysts collaborate interactively with an AI Business Analyst skill guided by the 11-Group Business Rubric—either starting from initial workshop notes or a partially filled form and interviewing only for the missing gaps, or walking through targeted commercial questions that quietly determine downstream architecture without technical terms (such as asking whether past figures should move when a customer changes segment rather than asking about dimension types). Whenever an answer is unknown, it is recorded explicitly as an open business question rather than guessed, producing a Business Requirements Document any executive sponsor can read, challenge, and stand behind.

Deterministic & Agentic Automation
  1. Deterministic Gate G0 validation checks that all 11 rubric groups and worked acceptance examples are complete, verifies every metric and attribute against the shared business glossary, mechanically rejects any leaked database or cloud vocabulary (such as fact table, SCD, partition, or BigQuery), and compiles any unanswered items into a plain-English follow-up question list for the business.
  2. An independent zero-context Ambiguity Check inspects the drafted Business Requirements Document on its own (without the interview conversation history) to surface hidden assumptions or unstated cancellation and restatement edge cases before sign-off.
Human Decision Gate

The business sponsor reads the Business Requirements Document written entirely in their own commercial language and signs off at Gate G0.

Step 3: Design — Ground Architecture and Approve the Design & 1-Page Playback (Gate G1)

Even the clearest business specification will stall in delivery if it collides six weeks later with an unexamined network firewall, an operational system that overwrites history in place, or three departments calculating the same metric differently. Traditionally, architects spend weeks in cross-team alignment meetings rediscovering enterprise standards and source quirks from scratch on every project—or engineers quietly compromise business requirements in code when a physical constraint surfaces mid-sprint.

Step 3 collapses weeks of architectural discovery into hours by resolving the technical design directly against the organisation's shared context registries—matching business intent against existing BigQuery foundations, conformed enterprise dimensions, security classifications, and real source reachability limits on Day 1. Whenever a source constraint requires a commercial trade-off, it is surfaced transparently to the sponsor upfront, and the resolved Design & 1-Page Playback is signed in plain English before build begins.

Deterministic & Agentic Automation
  1. Design & SQL skills combine the approved Business Requirements Document with Organisational Context to produce the Technical Design Document and Design & Product Manifest—defaulting to a lean direct model unless cross-domain conformance or historical tracking justifies dimensional or point-in-time modelling.
  2. Source feasibility checks compare requested freshness and history rules against registered source capabilities, surfacing physical constraints immediately rather than downgrading service levels silently during build.
  3. An independent Blind Playback agent inspects only the resolved manifest and SQL—without reading the original business document—and generates a one-page plain-English playback describing what the build will actually deliver, including any known source limitations.
Human Decision Gate

The business sponsor reviews the Design & 1-Page Playback and records their sign-off at Gate G1. DPF locks that signature to a cryptographic SHA-256 semantic digest covering grain, history, and metric definitions; if any subsequent edit alters business meaning, Gate G1 invalidates the signature automatically until re-confirmed.

Step 4: Build — Compile Pipelines, Security & BI Deterministically (Gates G2 & G3)

In conventional cloud delivery, signing off an architecture triggers the longest and most fragile phase of the project: weeks of manual coordination across separate repositories for transformation SQL, Terraform infrastructure, IAM and column masking, catalog registration, data quality rules, and BI semantic views. Every hand-off between data engineering, platform security, and analytics teams introduces subtle drift, inconsistent definitions, and multi-week review queues.

Step 4 eliminates manual multi-plane wiring by compiling every cloud layer deterministically from the signed manifest into Compiled Pipelines & BI. Engineers focus exclusively on genuine source-cleansing logic—and when exploring an unfamiliar dataset early on, can use a built-in Prototype Fast-Path to scaffold or import working Dataform and Looker sandboxes in minutes—while production promotion stays gated until business sign-off at Gates G0 and G1 is complete.

Deterministic & Agentic Automation
  1. At Gate G2, Skill & Adapter Compose selects the appropriate platform skills and MCP tools for each stage and verifies that every business acceptance scenario maps to an executable test.
  2. At Gate G3, the offline Deterministic Product Compiler renders the complete Dataform or dbt project, row-quarantine views, Terraform IAM and policy-tag modules, BigQuery Knowledge Catalog registration payloads, and Looker views and dashboards twice in memory to prove byte-identical determinism.
Human Decision Gate

The engineer writes only concise source-typing SQL where needed and approves the Compiled Pipelines & BI at Single Build Review (Gates G2 and G3), where every generated resource traces back to a numbered business requirement.

Step 5: Accept — Verify Live Data and Publish an Attested Live Data Product (Gate G4)

Every data leader knows the reputational cost of a pipeline that passes every technical continuous-integration check yet lands in an executive steering meeting showing revenue totals that disagree with Finance's general ledger or product categories that Merchandising no longer recognises. Passing SQL syntax and nullability checks proves that a pipeline ran; it does not prove that commercial truth was delivered.

Step 5 treats production publication as an earned commercial outcome rather than an automatic deployment step. Until a new or updated data product proves structural and scenario integrity against live data—and receives explicit sign-off from accountable domain authorities on the reconciliations no SQL query can verify alone—it remains marked provisional in BigQuery Knowledge Catalog so only an Attested Live Data Product ever reaches downstream consumers.

Deterministic & Agentic Automation
  1. Live Sandbox Verification executes every generated grain, history, quarantine, masking, sharing, LookML, and business-scenario SQL assertion against the deployed build, writing results into Build Digest Evidence bound to the exact SHA-256 build hash. Modifying even one line of SQL invalidates stale evidence immediately.
Human Decision Gate

For commercial truths that no SQL query can prove alone—such as confirming that monthly net sales reconcile to Finance's general ledger—domain owners record an explicit Outcome Attestation at Gate G4, promoting the Attested Live Data Product to published.

Step 6: Monitor & Evolve — Track Live SLA Status and Loop Amendments Back to Step 2

Launching a data product is only the start of its commercial lifecycle. Left to generic infrastructure monitoring, Day-two operations typically swing between two failure modes: alert fatigue from midnight cron checks firing on weekends when nobody is making decisions, or silent metric staleness during peak trading hours followed by weeks of ad-hoc ticket triage whenever an upstream schema shifts or a business rule evolves.

Step 6 anchors live observability and ongoing evolution in the exact business calendar and requirements agreed upfront—producing a continuous Live SLA Status & Change Brief that drives a fast virtuous cycle without sending existing products back through initial use-case scoring. Service levels are watched during real regional working hours, and every Day-two signal routes to the lightest gate that governs it: new metrics, altered commercial rules, or revised service levels loop straight back into Step 2 (Describe) for plain-English amendment in hours; purely technical optimisations with unchanged business meaning re-enter at Step 3 (Design); and only net-new spin-off ideas or low-adoption sunset reviews return to Step 1's portfolio funnel.

Deterministic & Agentic Automation
  1. Working-hours checks evaluate freshness and volume against the published data product strictly during the organisation's declared operating calendar—suppressing 3:00 AM Sunday false alarms while catching real business-hour delays.
  2. When a genuine breach occurs, DPF traces the alert straight back to the affected business requirement, links the operator runbook, and drafts a pre-populated Live SLA Status & Change Brief ready to enter Step 2 or Step 3 in hours.
Human Decision Gate

At Change & Evolution Triage, the product owner approves the Live SLA Status & Change Brief—deciding whether the resolution is an immediate operational replay, a plain-English requirement amendment into Step 2, or a technical tuning delta into Step 3.

3. Architectural Principles: From Business Intent & Enterprise Context to Four Synchronised Outcomes

Behind the six-step operating model sits a deliberate three-part architecture (Figure 2): combining per-product business intent with reusable enterprise context and approved architecture patterns (Column 1), decomposing business requirements into a multi-service technical design and deterministic compile plan (Column 2), and emitting four synchronised cloud outcomes with zero cross-layer drift (Column 3).

3.1 Business Intent & Enterprise Context

Ninety percent of architectural friction in enterprise data delivery comes from rediscovering institutional rules and reinventing service topologies from scratch on every project. Column 1 of Figure 2 separates what changes per product from the paved-road context that Enterprise and Platform Architects encode once and reuse everywhere:

  1. Business Specification (Per Product): Captures commercial purpose, required metrics, finest level of detail, historical behaviour, decision latency, data-fitness expectations, privacy boundaries, and worked acceptance examples in plain everyday English approved at Gate G0—never infrastructure or service names.
  2. Organisational Context (Encoded Once): Version-controlled registries encoding four enterprise assets: Approved Architecture & Semantic Patterns (sanctioned ingestion, processing, storage, semantic-layer, and serving blueprints paired with machine-readable entry conditions), the Shared Business Glossary (canonical metric definitions, Critical Data Element flags, and sensitivity classifications), Source System Limits (network reachability, event or change-capture capabilities, and whether operational tables retain history or overwrite in place), and Working-Hour Calendars & Sovereign Defaults (local trading hours and approved cloud regions).
  3. Existing Foundations (Reused): Catalogues established datasets, conformed enterprise dimensions across the conformance bus matrix, and existing governed semantic models so new products compose cleanly over trusted foundations rather than duplicating upstream pipelines or metric logic.

3.2 Grounded Design & Compilation

Business sponsors never specify event buses, stream processors, lakehouse table formats, or semantic join mechanics—they express operational and commercial needs. Column 2 of Figure 2 turns those plain-English needs into a concrete, multi-service Technical Design Document (TDD) and verified build across two stages:

Four architectural rules keep pattern decomposition rigorous rather than opaque:

  1. Per-stage pattern composition: Patterns are selected independently across five stages (Acquire -> Stage & Quarantine -> Transform -> Semantic Layer -> Serve). One BRD can combine event streaming for five-second alerts with analytical marts and a semantic layer for monthly Finance reporting off the same landing log.
  2. Non-prescriptive layer naming: Functional stages are decoupled from storage conventions. Teams using Medallion map Stage & Quarantine to Bronze and Transform to Silver and Gold in Organisational Context, while raw/conformed/mart, Data Vault, or zero-copy architectures keep their own naming.
  3. Complexity as a justified cost: Organisational Context defaults to the leanest viable pattern. Stepping up to stateful streaming, point-in-time history, or a multi-entity semantic model requires an explicit BRD signal—and any custom departure requires an Architecture Decision Record (ADR).
  4. Day-1 feasibility trade-offs: When a BRD requests sub-minute action from a source that only exports nightly batches, Step 3 flags the collision upfront—giving the sponsor a clear choice between funding source modernisation or amending the BRD before Gate G1.

Deterministic Product Compiler

Once the resolved TDD and one-page playback are signed at Gate G1, the offline compiler reads the signed manifest and concise transformation logic and renders the selected patterns twice in memory—guaranteeing byte-identical output and zero drift across data pipelines, security policies, catalog metadata, and semantic models.

3.3 Four Synchronised Outcomes

As shown in Column 3 of Figure 2, compiling the selected patterns from a single manifest produces four synchronised cloud outcomes simultaneously:

  1. Acquire, Stage & Transform: Emits the contracts and pipelines required by the selected data pattern—spanning event-stream schemas, append-only landing tables, blocking row-quarantine gates, point-in-time historical dimensions, restatement-bounded consumer marts, or multimodal enrichment workflows on Gemini Platform, mapped onto the organisation's chosen layer convention (such as Medallion, raw/conformed/mart, or direct views).
  2. Access, Masking & Sharing: Emits least-privilege IAM bindings, taxonomy classifications, column-level policy tags with dynamic PII masking rules, row-level access predicates, and isolated BigQuery sharing clean-room listings for external partners.
  3. Catalog, Lineage & Quality: Emits structured BigQuery Knowledge Catalog registration payloads carrying accountable ownership, business glossary links, upstream-to-downstream lineage expectations, and automated data quality gates.
  4. Semantic Layer & Consumption: Compiles the governed semantic layer—binding canonical metric formulas, symmetric-aggregate join keys, time-intelligence calendars, aggregate-awareness accelerations, Stephen Few-compliant executive dashboards, natural-language grounding for conversational AI agents, and operational API or partner serving contracts to the exact same underlying model.

4. Symbiotic Governance at Estate Scale: Pairing DPF with the Agentic Data Governance Framework (AGF)

4.1 What Is the Agentic Data Governance Framework (AGF) and How Does It Work with DPF?

The Agentic Data Governance Framework (AGF) is the companion runtime reference architecture that protects and governs an organisation's live data estate after products ship—and across all the ad-hoc analyst tables, raw landing zones, and third-party feeds that exist outside formal product pipelines.

Attempting to turn a build-time product compiler into an estate-wide runtime policing engine creates bloated, overlapping tooling. Conversely, running data governance as a manual review committee after pipelines are already in production fails because human stewards cannot keep up with cloud-scale data creation. As shown in Figure 3, DPF and AGF solve complementary halves of a single continuous lifecycle—DPF governing build-time specification and product delivery, and AGF governing run-time estate protection and continuous assurance across all fourteen EDM Council Cloud Data Management Capabilities (CDMC). Across its runtime checks, AGF applies an 80/20 operating principle: roughly eighty percent of routine scans, policy checks, and access expiries execute deterministically through event-driven rules, while bounded AI agents investigate the remaining twenty percent of ambiguous anomalies and route concise plain-English decisions to human stewards.

4.2 Data Product Framework (DPF): Build-Time Specification & Product Delivery

The left box of Figure 3 summarises how DPF embeds governance directly into product design before any pipeline reaches production:

  1. Plain-English Business Intent: Captures commercial rules, accountable ownership, data stewardship, and authorised business purposes in everyday language at Gate G0.
  2. Upfront Privacy & Policy Design: Applies shared business glossary definitions, enforces regional residency allowlists, attaches dynamic PII masking rules, and blocks any model that exposes restricted fields on an external partner boundary at Gate G1.
  3. Deterministic Product Compilation: Generates synchronised transformation pipelines, security policies, catalog contracts, quality assertions, and governed BI views from a single manifest at Gates G2 and G3.

4.3 Agentic Data Governance Framework (AGF): Run-Time Estate Protection & Continuous Assurance

The right box of Figure 3 summarises the three runtime assurance capabilities AGF operates continuously across the live data estate:

  1. Birth Protection & Live Lineage: Scans newly arriving or modified datasets for sensitive personal data, quarantines unowned assets before consumers can query them, links physical columns to the shared business glossary, and verifies that live pipeline and query activity matches approved design-time lineage.
  2. Purpose-Bound Access & Ledger Truth: Brokers time-limited consumer access (such as 180-day or 360-day grants) strictly against authorised business purposes while continuously reconciling critical financial figures against Finance's general ledger to supply automated evidence for Gate G4 sign-off.
  3. Retention & Shadow Asset Discovery: Enforces regional residency and legal holds, executes human-approved seven-year regulatory data retirement at end of life, and sweeps the broader estate to uncover ungoverned shadow pipelines and BI reports.

4.4 The Closed-Loop Handshake: Published Intent & Estate Feedback

As shown in the centre of Figure 3, DPF and AGF communicate with zero custom point-to-point integration through the shared catalog:

  1. Published Intent (DPF to AGF): Whenever a data product is compiled and published, DPF writes its ownership, authorised purposes, privacy rules, expected lineage, quality thresholds, and retention windows into BigQuery Knowledge Catalog—giving AGF an immediate machine-readable contract to enforce at runtime.
  2. Estate Feedback (AGF to DPF): Whenever AGF detects live lineage drift, newly arriving personal data in an upstream source, or a shadow pipeline gaining business adoption, it routes Estate Feedback back into DPF—either opening a pre-populated requirement amendment at Step 2 or onboarding the discovered shadow asset into the governed product lifecycle.

5. Adoption Roadmap & Reference Implementation

Organisations do not need a multi-quarter platform overhaul to adopt specification-driven data products. Because DPF compiles standard Dataform, dbt, Terraform, BigQuery Knowledge Catalog, and LookML files offline in Git, teams can adopt it incrementally across three pragmatic horizons:

  1. Horizon 1 — Seed Context & Deliver One Quick Win (Weeks 1 to 2): Initialise a clean DPF workspace, populate the core organisational context registries (working-hour calendar, initial source systems, and twenty to thirty glossary terms for one domain), score three candidate ideas at the Portfolio Gate, and take a single Quick Win from plain-English BRD through Gate G4.
  2. Horizon 2 — Standardise Multi-Plane Golden Copies & BI Guardrails (Weeks 3 to 6): Enable deterministic Golden Copy PR checks and the 26-case behavioural evaluation suite in continuous integration, bring Looker Explores and dashboards under the semantic compiler to eliminate shadow persistent derived tables, and expose shared domain dimensions as Existing Foundations for downstream decoupled products.
  3. Horizon 3 — Activate Symbiotic Estate Governance (Month 2 Onward): Turn on the seven ADG-00 BigQuery Knowledge Catalog aspect registrations and connect AGF runtime guardians on Gemini Platform to automate T=0 birth protection, expiring purpose-based access, general-ledger reconciliation, and continuous shadow-pipeline onboarding.

Conclusion: What This Architecture Proves

Enterprise data delivery was never slow because engineers typed SQL too slowly. Quarters disappear into unscored intake queues, translation drift between business sponsors and technical teams, weeks of manual stitching across five separate cloud tools, and late reconciliation failures when pipelines compile cleanly in continuous integration yet publish numbers that disagree with Finance. Simply generating code faster inside that broken process only accelerates rework and multi-plane drift.

As demonstrated throughout this whitepaper, compressing delivery from months to hours and days—while raising financial, privacy, and operational trust—requires four structural shifts working together:

  1. A one-time opportunity funnel feeding a continuous delivery flywheel: Candidate ideas from domain innovation workshops, revenue initiatives, cost-reduction programmes, and risk controls are scored once at the Portfolio Gate (Step 1) to fund high-return Quick Wins. Once live, ongoing business amendments loop directly from Step 6 (Monitor & Evolve) back into Step 2 (Describe) in hours rather than re-entering project intake.
  2. Plain-English business specifications grounded in reusable organisational context: Business sponsors capture commercial rules in everyday language (Gate G0) and sign a one-page plain-English playback locked to a SHA-256 semantic digest (Gate G1). Shared glossary definitions, source reachability limits, Existing Foundations, and regional working-hour calendars are encoded once in versioned registries so physical constraints—such as periodic outbound extracts from isolated subnets or source history starting at go-live—are resolved on Day 1 rather than discovered mid-build.
  3. Deterministic offline compilation across all four Google Cloud planes: From one validated manifest and concise staging SQL, the Multi-Plane Compiler emits byte-identical BigQuery and Dataform (or dbt) pipelines with row-level quarantine gates, Terraform IAM and dynamic contact masking, isolated partner BigQuery sharing listings, BigQuery Knowledge Catalog lineage and quality scans, and symmetric-aggregate-safe Looker Explores and dashboards in under two seconds (Gates G2 and G3).
  4. Digest-bound outcome attestation paired symbiotically with runtime estate guardians: Production publication is earned at Gate G4 by combining live BigQuery verification with explicit human domain attestation from Finance and business owners, all cryptographically bound to the current build digest. Rather than reinventing runtime policing inside a build compiler, DPF handshakes through BigQuery Knowledge Catalog and seven single-writer aspects with the Agentic Data Governance Framework (AGF) on Gemini Platform—sustaining all fourteen CDMC capabilities across table birth, purpose-gated access, ledger reconciliation, and seven-year retention.

Speed and governance are not a trade-off. When organisations automate multi-plane cloud plumbing deterministically and reserve human judgement for the six moments that define commercial meaning and truth, data products ship in days, evolve in hours, and remain trusted for years.
